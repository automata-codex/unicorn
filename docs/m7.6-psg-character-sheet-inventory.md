# M7.6 — PSG-Side Character Sheet Inventory

**Purpose.** Rules-authoritative input for the M7.6 spec: what the Mothership character
sheet *is*, per the source book, and where each value can change during play. Produced
without code access, deliberately — the current schema is the artifact M7.6 exists to
correct, so anchoring the target shape to it would encode the defect.

**This is a fidelity target, not a scope proposal.** The PSG's state model is
substantially larger than Phase 1 needs. Nothing here is a claim that it all ships in
M7.6. The spec's job is to draw a line through this list; this document's job is to make
sure the line is drawn knowingly.

**Source.** *Mothership Player's Survival Guide*, First Edition First Printing v1.2,
© 2023 Tuesday Knight Games — the file uploaded as `Player-Survival-Guide-v1_2.pdf`.
Citations use the PSG's own section numbering (`§18.1`, `§33.2`) where one exists, and
printed page numbers otherwise. **Confirm this file's SHA-256 matches the edition pinned
by the ingestion pipeline before treating the two as the same corpus.**

**Not covered.** The *Warden's Operations Manual* and *Shipbreaker's Toolkit* are
referenced by the PSG (hazard pay, WOM §48.2; space travel, SBT p. 30) and are out of
scope here. Nothing on the sheet appears to depend on them, but character-adjacent rules
may live there.

## 1. The classification that matters

The current architecture splits on **build vs. live mutable state**
(`decisions.md § Character sheet stores identity and build, not live mutable state`):
the sheet carries identity, build, and *ceilings*; current HP and current Stress live in
`campaign_state.data.resourcePools`.

Reading the PSG, that binary doesn't survive contact with the rules. **Live** survives
intact; **build** fractures into four. Five categories, not two:

| Category | Descends from | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Immutable** | build | Fixed at creation, never changes | Class, pronouns, creation rolls |
| **B — Mutable ceiling** | build | Presented as a cap, but the cap itself moves in play | Maximum Health, Maximum Wounds, Minimum Stress |
| **C — Mutable "build" value** | build | Currently classified as build; moves in play | All four Stats, all three Saves |
| **D — Item-derived** | build | Not a character field at all; a property of a stateful possession | Armor Points |
| **E — Live** | live | Changes continuously in play | Current Health, current Stress, Credits |

Categories B and C are the load-bearing findings. See §5.

**E is a classification, not a location claim.** HP and Stress are routed to
`resourcePools` today; whether Credits and High Score are on the sheet or in campaign
state is a code question for CC, not something the PSG can answer.

### 1.1 The rubric that exists, and the one that doesn't

Two different questions get conflated here, and only one of them has a recorded answer.

**"Which bucket *within* campaign state?" — answered, by shape.**
`MothershipCampaignStateSchema` is itself the rubric: `resourcePools` for per-entity
numerics keyed `{entity_id}_{pool_name}`; `entities` for visibility, status, and NPC
narrative state; `flags` for booleans bundled with their trigger; `scenarioState` for
*non-entity* numerics with a note; `worldFacts` for the prose scratchpad. Reinforced by
`decisions.md § HP and all numeric resources in resourcePools` — no special-cased numeric
fields, pool behaviour read from the system Zod schema.

**"Sheet or campaign state?" — never written as a general rule.** It exists only as a
single instance decision, `§ Character sheet stores identity and build, not live mutable
state`, which settles HP and Stress and asserts the identity/build/ceilings split as a
by-product. There is no entry a future field can be tested against.

**But that entry's *rationale* generalizes, and it is stricter than current practice.**
The stated reason is that `currentHp` and `stress` on the sheet "drifted from the
authoritative pool values the moment play began and served no purpose after creation."
Read as a rule — *if a value mutates in play, campaign state owns it; the sheet keeps
only what creation determines and play never touches* — it disposes of §5.2 immediately.
Maximum Health, Maximum Wounds, and Minimum Stress all mutate mid-adventure, so under the
project's own reasoning they are not sheet data. Same for all seven Stats and Saves.

That is worth noting precisely because it means §5.2 isn't a gap in the reasoning; it's a
gap in *application*. The rule was stated for two fields and never generalized, and the
fields it would have caught were classified before anyone read the Wounds table closely.

**Two frictions before adopting it literally.** `resourcePools` carries `{current, max}`
with threshold semantics the validator acts on — Stats and Saves have a max but no
threshold behaviour, so they'd be pools that never fire. And `scenarioState` is
explicitly *non-entity* numeric state, so it isn't the home either. Adopting the rule
consistently therefore forces either Stats-as-pools or a new bucket. That is Open
Question 2 in §6, and it's downstream of writing the rubric rather than a separate
choice.

**Resolved 2026-08-14.** The rubric is written, and it covers three destinations rather
than the two this section frames — the sheet/campaign line and the campaign/adventure
line are the same question asked twice, and answering them separately produces two
half-rules that don't compose. See `decisions.md § State placement is decided by the
lifetime of the referent, not the lifetime of the value`, with the sheet-specific
consequences as an addendum to `§ Character sheet stores identity and build, not live
mutable state`.

Under that rule, **every field M7.6 adds is campaign-scoped** — Mothership has no factory
reset, so nothing character-attached is adventure state. The mis-scoped state is all
*scenario* state (synthesized NPC and threat pools, flags, `worldFacts`,
`scenarioState`), which is not character-sheet work and does not belong to this
milestone. It is deferred to Phase 2 behind a single-adventure-per-campaign constraint
that makes the eventual migration unambiguous
(`decisions.md § One active adventure per campaign`, addendum;
`§ Adventure state gets its own row, not an adventure tag on campaign state`).

**Recommendation:** M7.6 writes the missing rubric into `decisions.md` as an addendum to
the existing entry, before the schema work — not after, and not as a by-product of it. A
rule stated once for two fields is what produced this milestone.

## 2. Core numeric fields

| Field | Creation | Cat. | Recovery | Where it moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 2d10+25, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | `-1d10` (Wounds: Fire 04, Gore 04, p. 29); radiation L2 `-1/round` (§33.2); Stress overflow >20 (§20.1); Military Training `-10` to one Stat (§25.1); restored 2d10 by Pseudoflesh (p. 35) |
| Speed | 2d10+25, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | Same as Strength; restored 2d10 by Pseudoflesh |
| Intellect | 2d10+25, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | Radiation L2; Stress overflow; restored **to maximum** by Psychosurgery (p. 35) |
| Combat | 2d10+25, ± class | **C** | **Paid, partial only** | Radiation L2; Stress overflow; `+10` from Military Training; restored 1d10 by Immersive Slicksim — **no restore-to-max exists** |
| Sanity Save | 2d10+10, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | Radiation L2; Stress overflow; `-1d5` on Slicksim mishap; restored 1 by Artificial Wellness Counselor, to max by Psychosurgery; **permanently raised** by Shore Leave (§39.1) |
| Fear Save | 2d10+10, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | As Sanity; restored 1d10 by Slicksim; raised by Shore Leave |
| Body Save | 2d10+10, ± class | **C** | **Paid** | `-2d10` (Wounds: Fire 05, Fire 06, p. 29); radiation L2; restored 2d10 by Pseudoflesh; raised by Shore Leave |
| Health (current) | = Maximum | **E** | **Rest** | Damage (§28.2); reset to Maximum on Wound and on successful rest Body Save (§34.1) |
| Health (**maximum**) | 1d10+10 | **B** | **None** | `-1d5` on Death table `00` (§29.2) |
| Wounds (current) | 0 | **E** | **Paid** | `+1` when Health hits zero; restored 1 by Medpod, all by Pseudoflesh. Explicitly *not* restored by rest (§34.1) |
| Wounds (**maximum**) | 2, +1 Marine, +1 Android | **B** | **None** | `-1` on Panic `19` Heart Attack / Short Circuit (p. 21) |
| Stress (current) | 2 | **E** | **Rest** — to Minimum, not zero | `+1` per failed Check/Save (§20.1); Panic and Wounds results; Rest Save reduces by ones digit (§20.2); Shore Leave resets to Minimum |
| Stress (**minimum**) | 2 | **B** | **Paid** | `+1` Panic 04/18/19, `+2` Panic 14, `+1` "Awesome scar" (Wounds Fire 01, Gore 01), `+1` pet killed (§11.1); `-1` Deep Tissue Nanogel; set to 2 by Psychosurgery |
| Stress **maximum** | — | **system constant** | n/a | 20, for all characters (§20.1). Overflow reduces the most relevant Stat or Save. |
| Credits | 2d10×10 (or 2d10×100 if forgoing loadout, §6.1) | **E** | n/a | Spent/earned freely |
| High Score | 0 | **E** | n/a | `+1` per session survived (§18.3); no mechanical effect by default |
| Armor Points | — | **D** | **Paid** (Patch Kit → AP 1; replacement) | Not a character field. See §4. |

**Three notes on the table.**

*"Restores to their maximum"* (Psychosurgery, p. 35) presupposes that Stats and Saves
have a maximum distinct from their current value. Nothing in the PSG names that maximum
explicitly; the natural reading is the post-creation, post-class-adjustment value. But
Shore Leave permanently raises Saves, so a Save's maximum is itself mutable. **Whether
the schema models a max per Stat/Save is a real design question, not a transcription
detail.**

*Both Wounds-maximum and Stress-minimum are labelled "Starts at 2"* on the character
profile sheet, which is easy to conflate when reading extracted text. They are separate
facts and both are true.

*The printed sheet gives Health, Wounds, and Stress a second box and gives Stats and
Saves only one — and that asymmetry must not be read as a rules statement.* The second
box is not "the maximum" in any uniform sense; it is **whatever value a mechanic snaps to
or compares against**:

| Field | Second box | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Maximum | **Reset target** — Health resets to it on a Wound and on a successful rest Body Save (§28.2, §34.1) |
| Stress | **Minimum** | **Floor** — rest reduction and Shore Leave snap back to it, never to zero (§20.2, §39.1) |
| Wounds | Maximum | **Threshold** — Wounds = Maximum fires a Death Save (§28.2) |

Stats and Saves have no reset target and no threshold; nothing in the core loop compares
current Strength to starting Strength, so the table never needs the number written down.
That Stress's box is labelled *Minimum* rather than *Maximum* is the tell.

**The concept exists anyway, and software cannot punt on it the way paper does.** Every
restore effect needs a ceiling to clamp against — Pseudoflesh restores 2d10 Strength,
Slicksim restores 1d10 Combat, Psychosurgery restores Intellect *to maximum*. Without a
stored ceiling, repeated treatments would push a Stat above its starting value. At a real
table the Warden remembers or rules it; a schema has to hold it. So **all seven values
need a ceiling, and only three have a mechanic that names one** (§5.6).

The general caution, which applies to this whole document: *the printed sheet is evidence
about what a table needs to write down, not about the data model.* Fields it omits may
still be required; fields it includes (High Score) may carry no mechanical weight at all.

## 4. Armor and equipment are stateful objects

This is the finding that makes `saves.armor: number` unfixable in place, and it is the
same fact that makes armor a `<character_attributes>` concern rather than static build
data.

**A worn armor item carries:**

- **AP** — but AP is *consumed*: taking damage ≥ AP in one hit **destroys the armor**
  (§28.3). A punctured vaccsuit decompresses in 1d5 rounds; a patched one has AP 1
  (Patch Kit, p. 11). So AP is `{ base, current, destroyed }`, not a scalar.
- **DR**, independent of AP and applied *first*, and surviving armor destruction and
  Anti-Armor weapons (§28.3). Advanced Battle Dress has DR 3.
- **O2 supply** with a duration (Vaccsuit 12 hrs, Hazard Suit 1 hr, ABD 1 hr) — and the
  Oxygen Tank halves to 4 hrs "under stressful circumstances" (p. 11). A depleting clock.
- **Speed penalty** — Vaccsuit and Advanced Battle Dress are Heavy: Speed Checks at `[-]`
  (pp. 14–15).
- **Integrated features** that change what checks are possible or modified: radiation
  shielding (blocks all three radiation levels, §33.2), air filter, exoskeletal weave
  (Strength Checks `[+]`), hydro reclamation, comms, headlamp, body cam, HUD.

**Weapons and consumables carry counts.** Loadouts are written as "Revolver (12 rounds)",
"Pulse Rifle (3 mags)", "Flamethrower (4 shots)", "Stimpak (x5)", "Patch Kit (x3)",
"Radiation Pills (x5)", "MRE (x7)" (p. 7). `equipment: string[]` preserves the *label* of
the count and none of the state. Several items also gate mechanics rather than just
existing — a crowbar grants `[+]` on Strength Checks to force airlocks; a Smart-link
Add-On adds +5 DMG; Stimpak use is dose-tracked over 24 hours with an overdose Death Save
(§33.3).

**Implication for the render bullets.** "Armor mode, weapon loadout, active conditions"
— the `<character_attributes>` triple — maps cleanly onto: *which armor is worn and what
state it's in*, *which weapon is readied and with how many shots*, and *the Conditions
list*. All three are qualitative-plus-counter, none is a scalar, and all three are
downstream of the item model above.

## 5. Where the PSG contradicts the current architecture

Five collisions, in descending order of how much they should change the spec.

### 5.1 Stats and Saves are not static build data

M7.6's second bullet is *"static character-sheet build data (stats, saves) into the
snapshot"*. Per §2, none of the seven values is static: Wounds reduce Strength and Body
Save, Level 2 radiation reduces all seven per round, Stress overflow above 20 reduces
"the most relevant Stat or Save", and five of the seven medical treatments restore them.

**Concrete consequence:** a render that treats these as build-time constants will hand
the Warden a stale target number after any Wound that reduced a Stat — and it will do so
silently, because the number will look plausible. That is a correctness bug the render
bullet would ship, and it's worth the spec saying so explicitly rather than leaving
"static" in the bullet's title to do quiet work.

### 5.2 Ceilings mutate, so "the sheet holds ceilings" doesn't hold

`decisions.md` places `maxHp` and `maxStress` on the sheet as build data and current
values in `resourcePools`. But Maximum Health drops 1d5 on a Death-table `00`, and
Maximum Wounds drops 1 on Panic `19`. Both are mid-adventure events.

Either the sheet becomes play-mutable — which contradicts the decision's own rationale,
that sheet values "drifted from the authoritative pool values the moment play began" —
or ceilings move into campaign state alongside the currents. **This needs a decision, and
it is upstream of every other bullet in the milestone.**

### 5.3 `maxStress` looks like a misnomer

The PSG has no per-character maximum Stress. 20 is a system constant (§20.1). What the
character actually carries is **Minimum** Stress, which starts at 2 and mutates in at
least seven distinct ways. If `maxStress` on the sheet is standing in for that, the field
is both misnamed and pointed at the wrong quantity.

### 5.4 Skills need suppression state

Panic `08` removes one Skill's bonus for the life of a Condition. A flat list of held
skills can't represent "has Hacking, currently getting no bonus from it", and the
suppression is a *link* between a Conditions entry and a Skills entry.

### 5.5 Several tracked states have no home on the sheet at all

Play-time character states the PSG defines mechanically, which are neither build data nor
obviously resource pools:

| State | Rule | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Bleeding** | §32.2 | Cumulative per-round damage, `+1` to `+7` from the Wounds table. Ignores armor and DR. A counter. |
| **On fire** | Wounds Fire 07/08; Flamethrower | 2d10 or 3d10 damage per round. A counter with a die expression. |
| **Pending Death Save** | Wounds Lethal Injury row | "Death Save in 1d10 rounds" — a timer. |
| **Cryosickness** | §32.3 | `[-]` on all rolls for 1 week. |
| **Exhaustion** | §32.4 | Body Save hourly after 12 hrs; `[-]` on all rolls after 24. |
| **Food / water deprivation** | §32.5 | `[-]` on all rolls. |
| **Radiation Level** | §33.2 vs. Radiation Pills (p. 11) | The pills say "reduce **your** Radiation Level by 1", implying a per-character tracked value; §33.2 defines levels as properties of the environment. **The rules are genuinely ambiguous here** — worth a house-rule decision recorded in `decisions.md` rather than an implementation guess. |
| **Temporary roll modifiers** | Panic 01/04/16/19, Stimpak | Duration-bounded `[+]`/`[-]` on all rolls, in minutes or hours. |

Most of these are timers or counters and plausibly belong in campaign state rather than
on the sheet. Listing them here so the spec chooses rather than discovers them at
playtest.

### 5.6 Mothership has no long rest, and the adventure boundary is where that breaks

**The rule.** There is no D&D-style long rest that restores a character to defaults.
§34.2 Long Term Recovery states directly that Wounds, Conditions, **and losses to Stats
and Saves** all take longer than a rest and points at the Medical Expenses table. Damage
to a Stat is a purchase to undo, not a night's sleep. Five recovery tiers exist, and two
of them are "buy it" and "never":

| Tier | Mechanism | Restores |
|---|---|---|
| Free, per day | 6+ hrs rest, Body Save (§34.1) | Health to Maximum, **on success only**. Wounds explicitly excluded |
| Free, per rest | Rest Save on worst Save (§20.2) | Stress by the ones digit rolled; **floors at Minimum Stress, not zero**; failure adds 1 instead |
| Paid, between contracts | Shore Leave (§39.1) | Stress to Minimum; converts some into *permanently improved* Saves |
| Paid, at a port | Medical treatments (p. 35) | The **only** path for Stats, Saves, Wounds, and Conditions |
| — | nothing in this book | **Maximum Health and Maximum Wounds** |

**The last row is the one that matters most.** Death-table `00` takes 1d5 off Maximum
Health; Panic `19` takes 1 off Maximum Wounds. Nothing restores either. Both category-B
ceilings are monotonically decreasing over a character's lifetime with no recovery path
at all — which is a deliberate design statement about attrition, and a schema fact: these
cannot be recomputed from creation rolls at any point after creation.

**Two asymmetries in the treatment table.** Only Intellect, Sanity Save, and Fear Save
can be restored *to maximum* (Psychosurgery). Strength, Speed, and Body Save get 2d10
chunks (Pseudoflesh); **Combat has no restore-to-max option anywhere in the book** — only
1d10 from Immersive Slicksim. And "maximum" is itself mutable: Shore Leave permanently
raises Saves, Military Training is `+10 Combat` with `-10` elsewhere. So a stored maximum
cannot be derived from the creation rolls either, for the same reason.

**Recovery horizon is a second axis, independent of A–E.** The categories in §1 classify
whether a value *moves*; they say nothing about whether it *comes back*. A field can be
category C and free-to-recover (none are), or category B and unrecoverable (two are). The
spec needs both axes, because the second one constrains storage in a way the first
doesn't.

**The concrete risk, and the thing to verify.** Because Stat damage survives an adventure
boundary, whatever holds it must too. `campaign_state` is campaign-scoped rather than
adventure-scoped, and `buildResourcePools` preserves keys already present at synthesis,
so the plumbing appears right — but **confirm that a second adventure in the same
campaign inherits a damaged Intellect rather than reseeding from the sheet.** If the
sheet is the reseed source and the sheet holds creation-time values, the system has an
accidental long rest at every adventure boundary, silently undoing exactly the attrition
Mothership is built around. This is a question for CC against the code, and it should be
answered before the rubric in §1.1 is written, because it is evidence about whether the
current split already leaks.

A related check for the same reason: `deriveMothershipCharacterResourcePools` seeds pools
"at full HP and zero stress from the ceilings" per `decisions.md`. **Zero stress is wrong
against the PSG** — current Stress starts at 2, not 0, and floors at Minimum Stress
thereafter, never at zero (§20.2). Worth confirming whether that phrasing describes the
code or just the decision entry.

### 5.7 Stat loss is unauditable, at two layers

Test question: *"My Strength is 27. I think it started at 42. How did I lose those
points?"* Today that is unanswerable, and it fails twice for different reasons.

**Layer one — there is no write path.** Stats are not resource pools. `resource_pools`
holds `{entity}_hp` and `{entity}_stress`; Stats live on the sheet as build data. A
Strength reduction from a Gore wound or from Level 2 radiation has nowhere to be
recorded, so the loss was never modeled in the first place. This is §5.1 restated as an
audit problem rather than a rendering one, and it closes the same way.

**Layer two — the delta carries no reason.** The tool schema is
`resource_pools?: Record<string, { delta: number }>`. A number and nothing else. Once
M7.6 routes Stats through pools, replaying deltas reconstructs *when* the points went;
*why* is recoverable only by reading the narration in the same `gm_response` payload and
correlating with adjacent `dice_roll` events. That is prose correlation, not a query —
and `UNAUDITABLE-MAPPING` is already a tag in the failure-mode taxonomy.

Three aggravating details:

- **Corrections.** A naive delta replay double-counts unless it filters `superseded_by`.
  Nothing enforces that; any reconstruction tooling has to know it independently.
- **No absolute is recorded per event.** Only deltas, so `sum(deltas)` should equal
  `current − initial` and there is no way to localize the drift when it doesn't.
- **Stress overflow is discretionary.** §20.1 reduces "the most relevant Stat or Save" by
  the excess above 20 — *the Warden chooses which*. A discretionary reduction with no
  recorded reason is the worst case for auditability, and it is a likely source of
  exactly the question above.

**The schema is already inconsistent about provenance.** `dice_requests` carry `purpose`;
`flag_triggers` carry no reason but their trigger is stored in state; `entities` changes
carry nothing; pool deltas carry nothing. Adding a `reason` (or `cause`) to pool deltas
would make HP and Stress loss auditable too, which is a larger win than the immediate
case.

**Why this is an M7.6 scheduling question and not a backlog item.** A field on
`state_changes.resource_pools` is a tool-schema change, so it needs matching Warden
prompt instructions and it invalidates the baseline. M7.6 already owns a re-baseline. The
precedent is exact: `rollType`/`gatedByRollId`/`actingEntityId` went onto `roll_dice` in
M7.5 as measurement infrastructure, batched with the prompt change and landed before that
milestone's re-baseline for precisely this reason
(`decisions.md § rollType / gatedByRollId / actingEntityId on roll_dice stay deferred, but
they are measurement infrastructure`). If delta provenance doesn't ride M7.6, it costs a
re-baseline of its own later — the same cost the batching discipline exists to avoid.

**Not a recommendation to include it.** It may be out of scope, and adding a free-text
field the Warden populates has its own failure modes worth weighing. The point is that
the *decision* belongs in M7.6, because M7.6 is where it is cheap.

## 6. Open questions for you, not for CC

These need a decision, not research. CC can't settle any of them from the code.

1. ~~**Write the sheet-vs-campaign-state rubric.**~~ **Resolved** — see §1.1. Recorded as
   `decisions.md § State placement is decided by the lifetime of the referent, not the
   lifetime of the value`. Every field M7.6 adds lands on the campaign side; the
   adventure-state split is Phase 2 behind the single-adventure constraint.

2. **Do Stats and Saves become resource pools?** They have min/max/threshold semantics,
   and the pool machinery already reads those from the system Zod schema. Against: seven
   more pools per character, and Stat Checks aren't threshold events in the way HP and
   Stress are.

3. **Are Conditions sheet fields, campaign-state entries, or entities?** They're
   parameterised, they have ongoing mechanical effects, and one of them mutates the
   Skills list.

4. **Are pets entities?** They have Wounds, Instinct, and skills, and they push Stress
   onto the owner. Modelling them as sheet fields duplicates the entity machinery.

5. **Does delta provenance ride this milestone?** (§5.7) A `reason` field on
   `state_changes.resource_pools` is what makes "how did I lose 15 Strength" answerable
   by query rather than by reading narration. It is a tool-schema change, so it needs
   prompt instructions and a re-baseline — both of which M7.6 already has. Deciding
   "no" is fine; deciding it later is what costs.

6. **How much of §5.5 is in scope for Phase 1?** Bleeding is common enough in real play
   to be load-bearing; cryosickness and food/water probably aren't.

7. **Migration disposability.** The pre-`v0.1.0` Flyway consolidation pass means this
   migration can be one-way and lossy if you want it to be — but that's a choice worth
   making explicitly, since existing `character_sheet` rows include the ones behind the
   captured fixture corpus.

## 7. What CC should produce alongside this

To make the diff computable rather than argued:

- The current `MothershipCharacterSheetSchema`, verbatim.
- Every reader of it — confirmed call sites, not inferred: `deriveMothershipCharacterResourcePools`, `formatMothershipCharacterProse`, both frontend character pages, plus anything else that touches `character_sheets.data`.
- The current `MothershipCampaignState` shape and the pool-definition metadata (`min`, `max`, `thresholds`) for the existing Mothership pools.
- Row count in `character_sheets`, and how many are referenced by captured fixtures.
- **Whether a second adventure in an existing campaign reseeds player pools from the sheet or inherits the damaged values** (§5.6). Trace `deriveMothershipCharacterResourcePools` and `mergePlayerResourcePools` against the synthesis path for a campaign that already has a character.
- Whether current Stress is actually seeded at 2 or at 0, and whether Rest-Save reduction floors at Minimum Stress or at zero (§5.6).
