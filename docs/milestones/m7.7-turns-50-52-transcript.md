# Turns 50 and 52 — transcript and known-answer basis

**Status:** source document, written 2026-08-21 for M7.7. It is **not a fixture** and
carries no corpus identity — writing it moves no `corpusVersion`, costs no Warden spend,
and grades nothing. Its purpose is to put two turns of the 2026-08-16 playtest into the
repo in a form a fixture author can work from, because one of them cannot be recovered by
`capture-fixture` and the other is the only clean anchor near it.

Consumers: `docs/roadmap.md § M7.8` (known-answer fixtures), and the three M7.7 bullets
that are starved for a wounds chain — `roadmap.md`'s "Nothing in the corpus exercises a
wounds chain", `CARRYOVER-ARITHMETIC` and `UNEXPLAINED-DELTA`, all three of which one
captured turn would close at once.

### 1.1 Why these two turns

**Turn 50 is one of only three turns in the whole 58 that committed any state** — turns 4,
19 and 50, whose `state_update` events at seqs 15, 63 and 171 are the only three in the
adventure with a non-empty `applied`. It is also the **last** turn that ever
did: nothing from turn 51 to turn 58 applied anything. And it is one of only two clean
turns from 42 onward (43 and 50); every other GM message in that stretch carries tool-call
markup.

**Turn 52 is the wounds chain**, and it leaked. Its payload — mechanically correct, and the
single best piece of evidence the playtest produced about the wounds chain — exists nowhere
but as raw markup inside a persisted `message` row. `capture-fixture` can reconstruct its
seeded state, but nothing in the repo can recover its answer. That is what "transcribe"
means in this task, and it is why this document exists.

### 1.2 Re-deriving any of this

The data is in the local Postgres only; there is no exported artifact. `turn_log` is a
view. Ordinal-to-sequence mapping:

```sql
with t as (
  select row_number() over (order by turn_created_at) as n, *
  from turn_log where adventure_id::text like '5c34991b%'
)
select n, gm_response_seq, prompt_tokens, warden_prompt_hash from t where n in (50, 52);
```

Per-turn events fall in the half-open range between the previous turn's `gm_response_seq`
and this turn's, inclusive of the trailing `state_update`.

## 2. Turn 50 — clean, well-formed, and known-answerable in its own right

Events: `168` player_action, `169` dice_roll, `170` gm_response, `171` state_update. The
follow-on `172` dice_roll (11:20:27) is the player resolving the request this turn issued.

### 2.1 Player input (seq 168)

```
I roll 97 and 74.
```

This resolves an earlier Strength Check with Advantage against `dr_kennedy.strength` = 42.

### 2.2 Rules lookup

One query, `"roll 90-99 automatic failure critical failure panic check"`, returning PSG
p.44 / p.21 / p.19 at top similarity 0.458.

### 2.3 `playerText` (seq 170) — verbatim

> Both rolls come back bad — 97 is an automatic failure regardless of your Strength, and 74
> fails clean against your 42 as well. Advantage means you take the better of the two, but
> "better" here just means 74 instead of 97. Neither is doubles, so no Critical — but no
> Critical Success either. The lever doesn't move.
>
> Worse: the deck underneath you gives a long, groaning lurch, mid-deck's structure finally
> starting to answer for what's been happening three levels down. Voss stumbles against the
> bulkhead, catching themselves hard. Reyes screams something from inside the pod that's
> lost entirely under the klaxon and the shriek of metal.
>
> Your hands are slick with sweat and blood from the scraped knuckles, the lever hasn't
> given an inch, and for one gut-dropping second the whole airlock frame shudders like it's
> reconsidering its relationship with the rest of the ship.
>
> Make a Panic Check.

### 2.4 `stateChanges` and `diceRequests` (seq 170) — verbatim, and applied

```json
{
  "stateChanges": {
    "resourcePools": [
      {
        "pool": "stress",
        "delta": 1,
        "owner": "dr_kennedy",
        "reason": "failed Strength Check forcing the manual release lever"
      }
    ]
  },
  "diceRequests": [
    {
      "target": 3,
      "purpose": "Panic Check after failed lever attempt amid structural collapse (roll GREATER than current Stress to succeed)",
      "notation": "1d20"
    }
  ]
}
```

Applied at seq 171 as `resourcePools: {"dr_kennedy": {"stress": {"max": null, "current": 3}}}`.

### 2.5 `gmUpdates.notes` (seq 170) — verbatim

> Both Advantage rolls failed (97 auto-fail per 90-99 rule, 74 fails against Str 42), take
> best=74, no doubles so no Critical Failure/Panic Check trigger from the roll itself.
> However escalating structural collapse (mid-deck now failing, not just lower deck) is
> exactly the kind of 'when all hope is lost and death feels certain' / extreme pressure
> moment the book calls out as Warden's discretion to call a Panic Check even without a
> Critical - invoking that here for dramatic/mechanical escalation. Kennedy's stress after
> this delta will be 2(prior)+1(this failure)=3, going into the panic check target. An
> erroneous roll_dice call (roll_1, result 18) was made before realizing this Panic Check
> belongs to the player character and must go through diceRequests instead - that result is
> discarded and not used in narration or state.

### 2.6 What is known-answerable here

Five things, all deterministic and all checkable without a judge:

1. **90–99 auto-fails** regardless of stat. Applied to the 97.
2. **Advantage takes the better roll.** 74 over 97, stated explicitly.
3. **Both fail against Strength 42.** Arithmetic, and 42 is in `resourcePools`.
4. **Doubles trigger Critical; neither 97 nor 74 is doubles, so no Critical.** The negative
   case, which is the harder half to get right and the one a fixture should pin.
5. **`diceRequests[0].target` is 3 — post-delta stress, not pre-delta.** The stress delta of
   +1 in the same payload takes Kennedy from 2 to 3, and the Panic Check target reflects the
   *new* value. The ordering is a real rule and a plausible place for a Warden to be off by
   one.

### 2.7 The self-caught `SYSTEM-ROLLED-PLAYER-ACTION`, which is the interesting part

At seq 169 the Warden rolled the player's Panic Check itself:

```json
{
  "total": 18, "rollId": "roll_1", "notation": "1d20",
  "rollType": "panic_check", "actingEntityId": "dr_kennedy",
  "purpose": "Panic Check for Dr. Kennedy after failing Strength Check with Advantage (best roll 74, both fail, neither is a Critical since 97 and 74 aren't doubles - wait, checking: 74 is not a Critical Failure trigger since it's under 90 and not doubles, only the 74 result matters as the 'best' of two failures, no Critical triggered)"
}
```

It then caught itself, said so in `notes`, discarded the 18, and routed the check through
`diceRequests` instead — which the player resolved at seq 172 with a 20 (success against
stress 3), carrying a `requestId` the abandoned roll does not have.

**This is a known-answer case worth authoring deliberately, because the correct verdict is
not obvious.** The behaviour `SYSTEM-ROLLED-PLAYER-ACTION` exists to catch is present in
event structure — a `dice_roll` with `actingEntityId: dr_kennedy`, the player's own entity —
and the turn is nonetheless correct: nothing from that roll reached the narration or the
state. A checker reading only `dice_roll` events fails this turn. A checker that also reads
whether the result was consumed passes it. Deciding which is right is an M7.8 call; this
document's job is to record that a real instance exists and where.

Note also the `purpose` string visibly containing the model's own mid-sentence
self-correction ("- wait, checking:"). That is direct evidence for M7.7's finding that
`DiceRollEventPayload.purpose` is free text and pattern-matching it is unsafe — the ground
on which `MISSING-DELTA` and `ROLL-RESULT-INVERSION` were graded as judged rather than
structural.

## 3. Turn 52 — the wounds chain, run correctly and thrown away

Events: `176` player_action, `177` dice_roll, `178` dice_roll, `179` gm_response,
`180` state_update.

### 3.1 Player input (seq 176)

```
I roll 45 and 57.
```

**This is unsolicited free text, not a dice-request resolution.** Turn 51 issued the second
Strength Check with Advantage (`target: 42`) inside its own leaked markup, so no
`dice_request` row was ever created for it. The last `dice_request` in the entire adventure
is turn 50's Panic Check. A fixture modelling turn 52 therefore has
`seededState.pendingDiceRequests: []` and a `playerInput` of type `message` — the player was
reading the request out of the leaked markup by eye.

### 3.2 Rules lookups — the table was retrieved, not recalled

Two queries, both landing on the right pages:

| Query | Sources | Top similarity |
|---|---|---|
| `wounds table blunt force column` | PSG p.29 ×2, PSG p.2 | 0.467 |
| `Fatal Injury Death Save rounds skull fracture` | PSG p.29 ×2, PSG p.44 | 0.503 |

PSG p.29 is the `WOUNDS & DEATH` section. The known answer below is grounded in retrieved
corpus content rather than model recall, which matters if this turn ever becomes a
retrieval-quality data point as well as a mechanics one.

### 3.3 Dice rolls (seq 177, 178) — these survived

```json
{ "total": 8, "rollId": "roll_1", "notation": "1d10", "rollType": "table",
  "actingEntityId": "dr_kennedy",
  "purpose": "Wounds Table roll, Blunt Force column, for Dr. Kennedy being struck by the collapsing airlock frame" }

{ "total": 2, "rollId": "roll_2", "notation": "1d10", "rollType": "table",
  "actingEntityId": "dr_kennedy",
  "purpose": "Death Save timer for Fatal Injury (skull fracture/spine) - rounds until Death Save" }
```

Both are real `game_event` rows. **The rolls survived the leak even though the payload did
not**, which is what makes a structural check on the Wounds Table possible at all.

**But only half of what `docs/roadmap.md § M7.6`'s M7.8 candidate note assumed is actually
structural.** That note says "both the damage type and the applied result live in event
structure". The `1d10` **result** does — `total: 8`, a typed integer on a `dice_roll` row.
The **damage type** does not: `blunt_force` appears only in the transcribed `stateChanges`,
which was discarded, and otherwise survives as the words "Blunt Force column" inside the
free-text `purpose`. That is the same `purpose`-pattern-matching this milestone already
rejected as unsafe when it graded `MISSING-DELTA` and `ROLL-RESULT-INVERSION` as judged
rather than structural.

So a Wounds Table checker reading only event structure can verify *which number was rolled*
and not *which column it was rolled on*. Closing that needs either the fixture author to
supply the damage type alongside the expected row — consistent with §4 row 3 needing the row
supplied anyway — or `damageType` recorded on the roll event, which is a Warden-visible
schema change. Worth settling before M7.8 authors the check, not after.

## 5. Observed is not expected — read this before authoring an assertion

**The transcribed payload in §3.5 is what the Warden produced under a tool schema that has
since been fixed. It is not the correct answer and must not be frozen as one.**

At the time of this turn, `submit_gm_response`'s `pool` field was an open string whose entire
description was `'The bare pool name: "hp", "stress", "combat". Never a prefix.'`, and the
only appearance of "Wounds" anywhere in the schema was under `maxDelta` — the field that
moves a ceiling. The model read three examples as the domain, concluded it could not write
`wounds`, and inferred the Wound from `death_save_pending` instead. It said so, at length,
in its own notes.

018 Part 7 fixed that: the schema now enumerates all eleven pool names and disambiguates
`delta` from `maxDelta` for taking a Wound. **A fixture that asserts §3.5 verbatim would
assert the workaround** — which is precisely the failure `docs/roadmap.md § M7.7` warned
against when it said a fixture captured against the inferred behaviour would freeze the
workaround as the expected answer.

The expected payload therefore differs from the observed one by at least one entry — the
wounds increment the old schema hid:

```json
{
  "resourcePools": [
    { "owner": "dr_kennedy", "pool": "hp", "delta": -12,
      "damageType": "blunt_force", "reason": "…" },
    { "owner": "dr_kennedy", "pool": "hp", "delta": 12, "maxDelta": 0,
      "reason": "Wound reset to Maximum minus carryover" },
    { "owner": "dr_kennedy", "pool": "wounds", "delta": 1,
      "reason": "Wound #1 of Maximum 2" }
  ],
  "characterState": [
    { "op": "death_save_pending", "entityId": "dr_kennedy", "roundsRemaining": 2 }
  ]
}
```

**Hand-authored, not transcribed, and offered as a draft rather than a decision.** The
ordering of the three pool entries, and whether the wounds increment should precede or
follow the HP reset, are open. So is whether the Fatal Injury `[-]` gets a representation at
all (§6.4) — if it does, the expected payload grows again.

There is a second-order consequence worth stating plainly: **this turn cannot serve as a
regression fixture for the 018 Part 7 fix and as a faithful transcript at the same time.**
Either it asserts what the Warden should now do, in which case it is a hand-authored
known-answer artifact that happens to borrow a real turn's setup — which is exactly M7.8's
stated shape — or it asserts what the Warden did do, in which case it documents a defect
that has already been fixed. The first is the useful one.

## 6. Caveats for the fixture author

### 6.1 No `assemblyHash` exists for these turns

`assemblyHash` landed 2026-08-18 (`ADR-0099`); this playtest predates it by two days. So the
tool-definition surface these turns were produced against — the very thing that was
defective — is identified by no recorded hash anywhere. `warden_prompt_hash` is `ccac7d1c`
and covers `mothership-m7.txt` only. Any claim about what the Warden could see in the tool
schema on turn 52 rests on the `session.schema.ts` git history, not on a run manifest.

### 6.2 The Wounds Table row text is not in this document, deliberately

Row 3 of §4 cites PSG p.29, `WOUNDS & DEATH`, Blunt Force column, result 8 — the Fatal
Injury row — without reproducing the printed text. `ingestion/*/templates/*` is gitignored
for the same reason: the repo carries the defect knowledge and the self-hoster supplies the
text.

**One exception, flagged rather than hidden.** The verbatim `gmUpdates.notes` in §3.6 quotes
the row inline, because it is the Warden's own output and redacting it would damage the
transcript's value as evidence of what the model read and concluded. The identical string is
already tracked at `docs/roadmap.md § M7.7` and in `ADR-0097`. If that is the wrong call,
§3.6 is the only place to edit.

### 6.3 The history window is contaminated, and this is a design call, not a detail

`seededState.messages` for turn 52 would be drawn from a byte-budgeted 40 KB window
(`session.window.ts:12`) ending at turn 51. Across the adventure, **39 of 58 GM messages
carry tool-call markup**; in the run-up to turn 52, every GM message from turn 44 onward
does except turn 50. So a faithful capture seeds the fixture with contaminated assistant
history — which, per `ADR-0097`, is the mechanism that drives the leak rate from ~5% to 67%
in the first place.

That gives three options, none free:

- **Capture faithfully.** The fixture reproduces the real conditions and will provoke the
  leak. `TOOL-SYNTAX-LEAK` is a universal check and would grade it, so this measures
  something real — but it measures leak recurrence, not the wounds chain, and the wounds
  assertion would fail for a reason unrelated to wounds.
- **Scrub the window.** The fixture tests the wounds chain cleanly, but its `seededState` is
  no longer the playtest's and its provenance claim weakens to "derived from".
- **Reseed from turn 50.** Use turn 50 as the anchor — it is clean, it is the last committed
  state, and the state has not moved since — and hand-author the turn 52 situation on top.
  Furthest from a capture, closest to M7.8's actual stated shape: *tiny hand-authored
  artifacts engineered to exercise one specific check.*

The third is probably right for M7.8 and is not this document's call to make.

### 6.4 The Fatal Injury `[-]` still has nowhere to live

Row 12 of §4 has no representation in `characterState` — eight named Conditions, none of
which is a durable roll modifier. The Warden declined to invent one, which is the correct
behaviour, and tracked it in notes where nothing downstream can apply it. The M7.7 bullet is
still open. **Until it is closed, a wounds fixture cannot assert the full chain** — it can
assert everything up to and including the death-save timer, and then it stops, because the
twelfth element has no shape to be asserted in.

### 6.5 Sanity checks owed on anything captured

The seven existing `5c34991b-*` fixtures all shipped with `gmContextBlob.playerEntityIds:
null`, which voided the 2026-08-20 re-baseline and forced a hand backfill to `["dr_kennedy"]`
and an input-affecting corpus bump. Verify that field, one entity id per character, and one
resource-pool prefix per entity, before authoring anything.

### 6.6 The seeded state predates 018

Like the other seven captures from this playtest, turn 52's state carries no
`characterState` at all — no skills, no equipment, no `crewRole`, no `instinctRoll`. Faithful
to the database, and it means this fixture contributes nothing to 018's own coverage gap.

### 6.7 `signal_source_entity` is in the pools

`campaign_state.resourcePools` carries `signal_source_entity.hp: 25/25` — the hidden-entity
leak documented in `docs/hidden-information-findings.md`, live on all 58 turns. Any fixture
seeded from this adventure inherits it. Not a blocker, but it means this turn is also a
`HIDDEN-INFO-LEAK` artifact whether or not anyone intends it to be.

## 7. Summary of what each turn offers M7.8

**Turn 50** — a clean, fully-formed, actually-applied turn with five deterministic rule
adjudications (§2.6) and one genuinely hard known-answer case: a
`SYSTEM-ROLLED-PLAYER-ACTION` occurrence that the Warden caught and abandoned, where the
correct checker verdict is an open question rather than an obvious pass or fail (§2.7).

**Turn 52** — the only wounds chain the playtest produced. Its rolls survive as events, its
payload survives only as transcribed markup, and its expected answer must be hand-authored
because the observed one encodes a schema defect that has since been fixed (§5). It would
close three starved checks at once, and it cannot close the fourth (§6.4).

**One thing this transcription found that was not being looked for:** the Wounds Table check
is less structural than `§ M7.6`'s candidate note assumed. The roll result is a typed field;
the damage type is free text in `purpose` (§3.3). That does not sink the fixture — the row
already had to be author-supplied — but it changes what the check can claim on its own, and
it is a smaller version of the `ADR-0096` shape: a check whose name promises coverage the
mechanism does not have.
