# M7.5 — Rules Retrieval Quality

**Spec status:** Claude Code handoff
**Depends on:** M7.2 complete — the ingestion pipeline runs end-to-end, `rules_chunk` is populated for Mothership, and the retrieval eval harness (`task eval:retrieval`) exists with a labeled fixture set. M7.3's `reconstructStateAsOfTurn` and M7.4's `eval:run` / `eval:compare` / `eval:capture-fixture` are needed for Parts 5 and 6 and have both shipped.

**Why this is a separate milestone from M7.2.** M7.2 ships the pipeline and the ruler; M7.5 uses the ruler. The two have different completion criteria in kind: M7.2's are binary (the CLI runs, rows land, the harness scores), M7.5's is a quality bar. Milestones with quality-bar criteria absorb whatever is adjacent to them, which is why the split exists — see `docs/decisions.md § Rules ingestion pipeline and retrieval quality are separate milestones`.

**What moved here from M7.2's spec.** Its Part 6 in full: the eval re-baseline (6.1) and the dedicated playtest (6.2). Both were deferred rather than dropped, because the re-baseline costs roughly 300 Warden turns plus judge calls and this milestone changes what is in the index. Buying it before chunking settles means buying it twice. The reasoning is preserved in `docs/plans/012-m7.2-rules-ingestion-implementation-plan.md § Moved to M7.5`; do not re-derive it.

## Goal

After M7.2 the index is populated and measurable, and that is all that is known about it. Nobody has established whether retrieval is good enough for the Warden to rely on, whether `rules_lookup` should refuse to answer when it has nothing good, or whether a populated index improves play at all.

M7.5 answers those three questions in that order, then buys the expensive Warden-level measurement once, against an index that is not going to change again immediately.

**What ships in M7.5:**

- A retrieval quality bar, set against M7.2's first measurement rather than guessed in advance, recorded in `docs/eval-methodology.md`.
- Chunking iteration against `task eval:retrieval` until the bar is met or the stopping rule fires — parameter sweeps and algorithm changes, each one measured, each one logged in `docs/rules-extraction-findings.md`.
- A similarity floor for `rules_lookup`, derived from the answerable/unanswerable distributions — **or** a recorded finding that the two distributions do not separate and no honest floor exists yet.
- `--markdown <path>` on `ingest.py`: a curated-input path that bypasses extraction. The capability ships; the curated file does not (see below).
- Fixup patches for Mothership, if and only if iteration shows extraction defects that patches fix better than chunking changes do.
- The eval re-baseline: full corpus, uniform N, both models, compared against the re-scored `88fa84bd8329` runs.
- The dedicated playtest against the finished index, reviewed with `task playtest:review`, with fixture capture for the four failure-mode tags still sitting at one instance.
- Resolution of the three `docs/decisions.md` entries that have been deferring to this measurement since M7.

**What does not ship (deferred by design):**

- **A curated Markdown of the Mothership PSG.** `docs/rules-ingestion.md § What ships in the repository` is explicit: "What does not ship: extracted Markdown, chunk text, or vector indexes for non-SRD systems." A hand-curated PSG is enriched extracted text and is the clearest case of that rule. If curation happens it lives on Alex's disk, is not version-controlled, and is not backed up by anything in this repo. **What ships is `--markdown`, the pipeline's ability to consume such a file.**
- The `roll_dice` schema fields (`rollType`, `gatedByRollId`, `actingEntityId`). Still deferred, still owed a baseline of their own — decided during M7.2 planning and not revisited here.
- Automated retrieval regression in CI. The harness needs a populated index and makes Voyage calls; neither belongs on a runner. Its *scorer* is unit-tested in CI already.
- Pre-built SRD indexes, non-Mothership systems, the query-embedding cache, a web ingestion UI, supplement text injection — all still Phase 2+, unchanged from M7.2.

## The stopping rule

This is the part of the milestone most likely to fail, and it fails by never ending. Chunking has an unbounded number of knobs and every one of them produces a number that could be a little better.

**The rule:** M7.5 closes when the quality bar set in Part 1 is met, **or** after three full iteration rounds that do not improve `recall@3` on the `authored` set by more than 5 percentage points in aggregate — whichever comes first. If the second condition fires, the milestone closes with the index as it stands, the bar restated as "not reached," and a `docs/decisions.md` entry recording what was tried and what the ceiling appears to be.

An unmet bar honestly recorded is a successful milestone. An unmet bar chased indefinitely is not.

**Corollary — freeze the fixture set.** The M7.2 fixture set is frozen at the start of Part 2 and does not change during iteration. Adding fixtures mid-iteration silently changes the denominator and makes round-over-round comparison meaningless, which is the same hazard `corpusVersion` exists to catch on the Warden side. If a fixture must be added or corrected, record it as a distinct measurement generation and re-measure the current best configuration under the new set before continuing.

## Part 1 — Set the bar

Cheap, and it has to come first, because a bar chosen after seeing the results you want is not a bar.

Run `task eval:retrieval` against the M7.2 index. That report is the baseline. From it, set targets for:

- `recall@3` on `authored` — the Warden's default `limit` is 3, so this is the number that matters most.
- `recall@3` on `warden-observed` — expected to be lower; the real recorded queries are keyword-stuffed and fuzzy (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md`). Set this target separately and do not average the two.
- `MRR` on the answerable set.

Record the bar and its justification in `docs/eval-methodology.md`, alongside the Warden-harness methodology rather than in a separate document — the two are different measurements but the same discipline about what a number means.

**Do not** set a `recall@5` target. It is worth reporting, but tuning against it optimizes for a `limit` the Warden rarely uses.

## Part 2 — Chunking iteration

The core of the milestone. Each round: change one thing, re-ingest, re-score, log.

Candidate levers, roughly in order of expected value per unit effort:

1. **Chunk size and overlap.** `target_tokens` and the 50–100 token overlap band are inherited heuristics from `docs/rules-ingestion.md § Step 4`, never validated. A sweep is cheap.
2. **Breadcrumb composition.** M7.2 prepends the footer chapter. Whether adding marker's immediate `SectionHeader` helps or adds noise is measurable, and the answer is not obvious given how unreliable that field proved (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S1.7`).
3. **Table handling.** M7.2 never splits a `Table` block. Whether an oversized table should instead be split by rows with a repeated header is a real question for a book whose most-queried content is tables.
4. **The footer-less pages.** M7.2 left the policy for the 8 unresolved pages (18% of the book's text) as an open question with a placeholder default. They include genuine body content and duplicated reference cards; dropping, attributing, and deduping are all plausible and all measurable.
5. **Duplicate reference spreads.** Physical pages 41/42 duplicate page 4, and pages 1 and 43 duplicate body rules as reference cards. Near-duplicate chunks compete in cosine ranking. Deduping is measurable.

**One change per round.** Two changes and a moved number tells you nothing about which one moved it — the same discipline the re-baseline itself is held to.

**Record index provenance every round.** M7.2's harness writes marker version, chunking parameters, embed model, and chunk count into the run manifest. A score compared across different provenance is not a comparison.

## Part 3 — Curated Markdown input (`--markdown`)

**Treat curation as a fallback the pipeline supports, not the plan of record.** If Part 2 reaches the bar, this part ships the capability and nothing else. Curation is what you reach for when measurement says automation is not enough.

### 3.1 The capability

`ingest.py --system mothership --markdown <path>` skips extraction and hash verification and feeds the file into the same chunking, embedding, and storage path. Two consequences worth stating:

- **It decouples Alex's index from marker.** The `marker-pdf` pin stays load-bearing for every self-hoster on the PDF path; it stops being load-bearing for a curated index.
- **It makes the pipeline end-to-end testable without marker for the first time.** A small synthetic curated file exercises chunk → embed → store with no model weights. Whether that becomes a real test (with a mocked embedder) or stays a manual affordance is an implementation call, but the option did not exist before this part.

The curated file needs the structure marker could not supply: a real heading hierarchy, and page markers in whatever form `chunk.py` already consumes. Document the expected format in `ingestion/README.md` precisely enough that a self-hoster could produce one for their own book — that is the entire shipped value of this part.

### 3.2 Establish the ceiling before committing to the work

Curating a 44-page book by hand is an evening at best. Before spending it, **curate two or three chapters only** — ideally `PANIC CHECKS`, `STAT CHECKS & SAVES`, and one table-heavy chapter — and score the fixtures that target those chapters against both the curated and automated versions.

That gap is the ceiling: it says how much of the remaining shortfall is chunking quality versus something else entirely (embedding model, query phrasing, the fixture labels themselves). If the gap is small, full curation is not worth the evening and Part 2's levers are the better investment. If it is large, you now have a measured reason to spend it.

This experiment is the recommended path even if you expect to curate anyway, because it is also the only way to distinguish "our chunking is bad" from "this book is hard to retrieve from."

## Part 4 — The similarity floor

**Sequencing: this must land before Part 5.** It changes what reaches the Warden, which invalidates frozen artifacts. Landing it after the re-baseline means paying for another one.

M7.2 established that `rules_lookup` has no floor: `RulesLookupService.lookup()` returns whatever `findByCosineSimilarity` gives back, and the SQL is `ORDER BY … LIMIT n` with no threshold. On a populated index the Warden therefore receives three chunks for every question, including questions the PSG cannot answer.

Use the top-1 similarity distributions from the `answerable: true` and `answerable: false` fixtures. If they separate, the floor goes between them; implement it in the service (not the SQL — keeping it in TypeScript makes it testable without a database and visible to anyone reading the service). If they overlap, **do not invent a threshold**: record the overlap in `docs/decisions.md` and leave the behavior unchanged. A floor that discards good chunks is worse than none, and the empty-result path the Warden already handles correctly is the safe default either way.

If a floor lands, note that it changes the *rate* of empty results the Warden sees, which is exactly the kind of behavioral shift Part 5 is measuring. That is intended and is why the ordering is fixed — but say so in the re-baseline's write-up, because the run then has two intended variables (populated index, plus a floor) rather than one, and a reader deserves to know that before attributing a movement.

## Part 5 — Eval re-baseline

Inherited from M7.2's spec Part 6.1. Full corpus, uniform N (10 as of the 2026-07-29 calibration — keep it uniform, per `docs/eval-methodology.md`'s adaptive-N hazard), both models (`claude-sonnet-4-6` and `claude-sonnet-5`), compared against the re-scored `88fa84bd8329` runs `claude-sonnet-4-6__97feadbd__2026-07-29T10-51-26Z` and `claude-sonnet-5__97feadbd__2026-07-29T15-40-17Z`.

**Run before Part 6's fixtures land.** `corpusVersion` is a content hash over every file in `eval/fixtures/` (`eval/corpus-version.ts`), and `eval:compare` warns on any cross-corpus pairing — deliberately, because that is the silent-poisoning case the hash exists to prevent. Baseline against the corpus as it stands, then capture.

**Verify the index is populated in the database the harness points at**, before spending anything. Structurally this holds — `eval:run` uses `--env-file=.env` and its scratch adventure resolves the same `mothership` `game_system` row ingestion writes under — but a re-baseline against an empty index measures nothing and looks exactly like a successful run. `task eval:retrieval` against the same `.env` is the cheapest possible check and should be the last thing done before the spend.

**The comparison is index-vs-index, not model-vs-model.** Same prompt hash, same corpus, same N, same grader.

Update `Current baseline N` in `docs/eval-methodology.md` if applicability or variance shifted. Rules availability plausibly changes reach-for-dice behaviour, which moves applicability denominators on the roll-shaped checks.

## Part 6 — Dedicated playtest and fixture capture

Inherited from M7.2's spec Part 6.2. A real Mothership session against the final index, reviewed with `task playtest:review`.

It answers two questions the harness cannot. **Did the index actually help?** — the harness replays frozen turns from adventures that ran without rules; it can show whether behaviour on those turns changed, not whether a live session goes better. **Does it supply the fixtures M7.4 is still waiting on?** — `MISSING-CANON-CAPTURE`, `UNSURFACED-CHECK`, `OVER-RESOLUTION`, and `SCENE-JUMP` each sit at a single fixture. Capture with `task eval:capture-fixture`.

Read the `rulesLookups` telemetry specifically. This is the first playtest in the project's history where those lookups return anything, so it is also the first real evidence about query-style distribution — feed anything surprising back into the retrieval fixture set as new `warden-observed` entries, *after* the milestone closes rather than during Part 2's frozen window.

**One caution, and it is the point rather than boilerplate.** A single session is not guaranteed to exhibit all four failure modes, and a fixture authored from a turn that did not actually exhibit its tag is worse than a missing fixture — it pins a check to behaviour that was never wrong, permanently and invisibly. If a tag stays at one instance, that is evidence about how rare the mode is: record it, and leave M7.4's fixture bullet open rather than closing it with a manufactured instance.

## Testing Summary

**Automated (CI):**

- Retrieval scorer unit tests and fixture-schema tests, from M7.2 — extended if Part 2 changes the scoring surface.
- `ingestion/tests/test_chunk.py` — extended for every chunking change Part 2 lands. A chunking change without a corresponding test change should be suspicious.
- Similarity-floor filtering, if Part 4 lands one: unit tests over `RulesLookupService` covering above-floor, below-floor, all-below-floor (empty result), and boundary.

**Measured, not tested (local only):**

- `task eval:retrieval` per iteration round. Not a pass/fail test — it has no threshold until Part 1 sets one, and even then the bar is a milestone criterion rather than a build gate.
- The re-baseline and the playtest, per Parts 5 and 6.

**Not tested:** marker extraction accuracy, Voyage embedding, the curated Markdown's fidelity to the book (a human transcription check, not an automated one).

## Deferrals Introduced in M7.5

### Embedding model upgrade as a retrieval lever

`voyage-4` and `voyage-4-large` are the documented step-ups from `voyage-4-lite` (`docs/rules-ingestion.md § Step 5`), all three at 1024 dimensions, so a swap is a config change rather than a migration. It is deliberately not a Part 2 lever: it changes the runtime `VOYAGE_EMBED_MODEL` as well as ingestion, costs a full re-embed, and forces its own Warden re-baseline. Revisit if chunking work plateaus below the bar.

### A second curated book

If curation proves its value on the PSG, the Warden's Operations Manual is the obvious next candidate. Out of scope here — one curated book is enough to learn whether the approach is worth generalizing.

### Retrieval fixtures for non-Mothership systems

The fixture set is Mothership-specific by construction (page labels, chapter names). A second system needs its own set, authored the same way. Not a reusable asset, and not a reason to delay this one.

### Automated chunk-quality regression

Once a bar exists and is met, a regression check that fails when retrieval degrades becomes conceivable. It needs a populated index and Voyage calls, so it cannot be a CI job as things stand — it would need a cached-embedding fixture harness, which is its own design problem. Revisit if chunking changes become frequent enough that manual re-scoring is the bottleneck.
