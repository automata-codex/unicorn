# M7.5 — Rules Retrieval Quality

**Spec status:** Claude Code handoff
**Depends on:** M7.2 complete — the ingestion pipeline runs end-to-end, `rules_chunk` is populated for Mothership, and the retrieval eval harness (`task eval:retrieval`) exists with a labeled fixture set. M7.3's `reconstructStateAsOfTurn` and M7.4's `eval:run` / `eval:compare` / `eval:capture-fixture` are needed for Parts 4.5, 5, and 6 and have both shipped.

**Why this is a separate milestone from M7.2.** M7.2 ships the pipeline and the ruler; M7.5 uses the ruler. The two have different completion criteria in kind: M7.2's are binary (the CLI runs, rows land, the harness scores), M7.5's is a quality bar. Milestones with quality-bar criteria absorb whatever is adjacent to them, which is why the split exists — see `docs/decisions.md § Rules ingestion pipeline and retrieval quality are separate milestones`.

**What moved here from M7.2's spec.** Its Part 6 in full: the eval re-baseline (6.1) and the dedicated playtest (6.2). Both were deferred rather than dropped, because the re-baseline costs roughly 300 Warden turns plus judge calls and this milestone changes what is in the index. Buying it before chunking settles means buying it twice. The reasoning is preserved in `docs/decisions.md § Rules ingestion pipeline and retrieval quality are separate milestones`; do not re-derive it.

## Goal

After M7.2 the index is populated and measurable, and that is all that is known about it. Nobody has established whether retrieval is good enough for the Warden to rely on, whether `rules_lookup` should refuse to answer when it has nothing good, or whether a populated index improves play at all.

M7.5 answers those three questions in that order, then buys the expensive Warden-level measurement once, against an index that is not going to change again immediately.

**What ships in M7.5:**

- A retrieval quality bar, set against M7.2's first measurement rather than guessed in advance, recorded in `docs/eval-methodology.md`.
- Chunking iteration against `task eval:retrieval` until the bar is met or the stopping rule fires — parameter sweeps and algorithm changes, each one measured, each one logged in `docs/rules-extraction-findings.md`.
- A similarity floor for `rules_lookup`, derived from the answerable/unanswerable distributions — **or** a recorded finding that the two distributions do not separate and no honest floor exists yet.
- `--markdown <path>` on `ingest.py`: a curated-input path that bypasses extraction. The capability ships; the curated file does not (see below).
- Fixup patches for Mothership, if and only if iteration shows extraction defects that patches fix better than chunking changes do.
- The `rollType` / `gatedByRollId` / `actingEntityId` fields on `roll_dice`, plus Warden prompt instructions for populating them — see Part 4.5. Landing here, not deferred further: per `docs/decisions.md § rollType / gatedByRollId / actingEntityId on roll_dice stay deferred, but they are measurement infrastructure`, a field the prompt doesn't instruct Claude to populate would still read `not_applicable` at re-baseline, forcing a third one later — exactly the repeated cost the M7.2/M7.5 split exists to avoid paying twice already.
- **Two Warden-side query-quality levers, and the instrument that measures them** — vocabulary bridging and the mechanical-model primer, both prompt-only, plus an out-of-corpus query-term score over the queries the Warden actually emits. See Part 4.6. These are `docs/roadmap.md` M7.5 bullets that the first draft of this spec omitted; they are in scope for the same sequencing reason as Parts 4 and 4.5 — they change the Warden prompt, so landing them after the re-baseline means buying another one.
- The eval re-baseline: full corpus, uniform N, both models, compared against the re-scored `88fa84bd8329` runs.
- The dedicated playtest against the finished index, reviewed with `task playtest:review`, with fixture capture for the four failure-mode tags still sitting at one instance.
- Resolution of the three `docs/decisions.md` entries that have been deferring to this measurement since M7.

**What does not ship (deferred by design):**

- **A curated Markdown of the Mothership PSG.** `docs/rules-ingestion.md § What ships in the repository` is explicit: "What does not ship: extracted text (Markdown or block output), chunk text, or vector indexes for non-SRD systems." A hand-curated PSG is enriched extracted text and is the clearest case of that rule. If curation happens it lives on Alex's disk, is not version-controlled, and is not backed up by anything in this repo. **What ships is `--markdown`, the pipeline's ability to consume such a file.**
- Automated retrieval regression in CI. The harness needs a populated index and makes Voyage calls; neither belongs on a runner. Its *scorer* is unit-tested in CI already.
- Pre-built SRD indexes, non-Mothership systems, the query-embedding cache, a web ingestion UI, supplement text injection — all still Phase 2+, unchanged from M7.2.

## The stopping rule

This is the part of the milestone most likely to fail, and it fails by never ending. Chunking has an unbounded number of knobs and every one of them produces a number that could be a little better.

**The rule:** M7.5 closes when the quality bar set in Part 1 is met, **or** after three full iteration rounds that do not improve `recall@3` by more than 5 percentage points in aggregate — whichever comes first. If the second condition fires, the milestone closes with the index as it stands, the bar restated as "not reached," and a `docs/decisions.md` entry recording what was tried and what the ceiling appears to be.

**Which `recall@3`, corrected.** This rule originally named the `authored` set. That was wrong on arithmetic: M7.2 measured `authored` at **100.0%**, so it cannot improve by any amount, and the second condition would fire after round three no matter what happened — including a round that moved `warden-observed` from 91.3% to 100%. The no-progress test is therefore evaluated on `recall@3` over the **answerable set as a whole** and on `warden-observed` specifically — the metrics with headroom — with `authored` held as a **regression floor** rather than a growth target. The 5 pp threshold and the three-round budget are unchanged; only the axis moves. A round that drops `authored` below 100% has made things worse regardless of what it did elsewhere, and is logged as such.

An unmet bar honestly recorded is a successful milestone. An unmet bar chased indefinitely is not.

**Corollary — freeze the fixture set.** The M7.2 fixture set is frozen at the start of Part 2 and does not change during iteration. Adding fixtures mid-iteration silently changes the denominator and makes round-over-round comparison meaningless, which is the same hazard `corpusVersion` exists to catch on the Warden side. If a fixture must be added or corrected, record it as a distinct measurement generation and re-measure the current best configuration under the new set before continuing.

## Part 1 — Set the bar

Cheap, and it has to come first, because a bar chosen after seeing the results you want is not a bar.

Run `task eval:retrieval` against the M7.2 index. That report is the baseline. From it, set targets for:

- `recall@3` on `authored` — the Warden's default `limit` is 3, so this is the number that matters most.
- `recall@3` on `warden-observed` — expected to be lower; the real recorded queries are keyword-stuffed and fuzzy (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md`). Set this target separately and do not average the two.
- `MRR` on the answerable set.

Record the bar and its justification in `docs/eval-methodology.md`, alongside the Warden-harness methodology rather than in a separate document — the two are different measurements but the same discipline about what a number means.

**Do not** set a `recall@5` target. It is worth reporting, but tuning against it optimizes for a `limit` the Warden rarely uses.

## Part 2 — Chunking iteration

The core of the milestone. Each round: change one thing, re-ingest, re-score, log.

Candidate levers below, roughly in order of expected value per unit effort. **This is a list of examples, not an allowlist** — see [2.1](#21-adding-a-strategy-not-listed-here) for how to bring in a strategy that is not on it.

1. **Chunk size and overlap.** `target_tokens` and the 50–100 token overlap band are inherited heuristics from `docs/rules-ingestion.md § Step 4`, never validated. A sweep is cheap.
2. **Breadcrumb composition.** M7.2 prepends the footer chapter. Whether adding marker's immediate `SectionHeader` helps or adds noise is measurable, and the answer is not obvious given how unreliable that field proved (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S1.7`).
3. **`SectionHeader` blocks as indexable content, not just breadcrumb material.** Distinct from lever 2 — that's about enriching an existing chunk's prefix; this is whether standalone heading text should be embedded as its own retrievable unit at all. All 169 `SectionHeader` blocks (2,632 characters) are currently excluded from the corpus outright, and there's a confirmed cost: `surprise` scores as absent from the book across every query that uses it, even though the PSG prints a `26.2 SURPRISE` section — the term exists only in a heading no chunk contains (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S9.1`).
4. **Table handling.** M7.2 never splits a `Table` block. Whether an oversized table should instead be split by rows with a repeated header is a real question for a book whose most-queried content is tables.
5. **The footer-less pages.** M7.2 left the policy for the remaining unresolved pages as an open question with a placeholder default. **5 pages, not 8** — physical 0, 1, 2, 10, 43, roughly 11% of the book's text. Pages 4, 41, and 42 are not part of this lever: they're already excluded outright under the settled character-creation exclusion (`docs/decisions.md § Character-creation content is excluded from the rules index`), not merely unresolved like these five. Of the five, page 10 (equipment continuation) is genuine body content and the one most likely to matter for retrieval; pages 1 and 43 duplicate live body rules as reference cards (lever 6); 0 and 2 are cover/credits and plausible candidates to drop the same way character creation was, but not yet checked with the same rigor.
6. **Duplicate reference spreads.** Pages 1 and 43 duplicate body rules as reference cards — the only genuinely open dedup question left. Pages 41/42 duplicate page 4 and are not a dedup question at all; they're already resolved by the exclusion in lever 5's note above. Near-duplicate chunks compete in cosine ranking. Deduping 1/43 is measurable.
7. **Page 3.** The character-profile sheet — `docs/decisions.md` flags it as probably the same unreachable category as pages 4/41/42, but the analysis that confirmed those three never covered page 3 directly. Left in, it's a measured false-positive risk: ranked top-3 for two of three test queries on stat-name density alone, with no real relevance to what was asked (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S3.7`). Cheapest lever on this list — confirm and exclude, or confirm it's genuinely different from the other three and say why.

**One change per round.** Two changes and a moved number tells you nothing about which one moved it — the same discipline the re-baseline itself is held to.

**Record index provenance every round.** M7.2's harness writes marker version, chunking parameters, embed model, and chunk count into the run manifest. A score compared across different provenance is not a comparison.

### 2.1 Adding a strategy not listed here

The seven levers above share a hidden assumption: each is a knob on one architecture — marker blocks → merge toward a token target → breadcrumb → store. The measurement apparatus around them does **not** share that assumption. Fixtures, page-label scoring, `recall@3`/MRR, the provenance manifest, the findings log, and the stopping rule know nothing about how chunks were produced, and the stopping rule is phrased in outcomes rather than in "the listed levers were exhausted."

So a new strategy is free to join the rotation, provided it is triaged first. Run these five questions in order:

| If the strategy… | Then | Amend this spec? |
|---|---|---|
| Only changes how blocks become chunks | Drop-in lever | **No.** Add a findings session and extend `test_chunk.py` |
| Changes `rules_chunk`'s shape — e.g. hybrid BM25 needs a `tsvector` column and index; parent-child retrieval needs a second table or an extra column | Contradicts this spec's "no migration, no `docs/schema.md` change" | **Yes** |
| Changes query time — query rewriting, HyDE, reranking | Is not chunking at all, and changes what reaches the Warden | **Yes**, and it inherits Part 4's must-land-before-Part-5 ordering |
| Adds an LLM call — chunk summaries, synthetic questions, LLM-assisted structure repair | Puts Anthropic in a pipeline specified as offline and Voyage-only; new cost model; derived-text licensing question under `docs/rules-ingestion.md § Licensing Posture` | **Yes** |
| Adds a runtime hop — cross-encoder rerank | `docs/rules-ingestion.md § Query Time` budgets 100–200 ms for the Voyage call in the GM turn hot path | **Yes** |
| Changes what a "correct" result *is* | The metric breaks before the strategy can be judged — see below | **Yes**, and the metric moves first |

**The last row is the one that fails quietly, so read it twice.** `recall@3` and MRR answer "did the right chunk come back." They do not answer "was the returned text sufficient to adjudicate," which is what actually matters to the Warden. For the seven listed levers those two questions move together, so the cheap deterministic metric is a fair proxy. For a strategy like parent-child retrieval or LLM-generated summaries they come apart: a summary chunk can score badly on page-label recall while serving the Warden better, and a large parent chunk can score well while burying the answer three paragraphs down.

Measuring sufficiency needs a judge, and M7.2 deliberately kept judges out of the retrieval harness to make it cheap enough to run every round (`docs/specs/zoltar/012-m7.2-rules-ingestion.md § Part 8.1`). That was the right call for parameter sweeps and it is a real constraint on strategy comparison. A strategy in this category must have its metric question settled **before** it is scored — otherwise it produces a number, the number is wrong, and nothing about it looks wrong.

**Amending is cheap; skipping the triage is not.** "Amend this spec" mostly means a paragraph in Part 2 and a line in the Documentation PR Checklist. The cost being guarded against is a strategy that quietly crosses a boundary — a migration that was declared out of scope, an API call in an offline pipeline, a latency regression in the turn hot path — and is discovered after the index has been rebuilt around it.

## Part 3 — Curated Markdown input (`--markdown`)

**Treat curation as a fallback the pipeline supports, not the plan of record.** If Part 2 reaches the bar, this part ships the capability and nothing else. Curation is what you reach for when measurement says automation is not enough.

### 3.1 The capability

`ingest.py --system mothership --markdown <path>` skips extraction and hash verification and feeds the file into the same chunking, embedding, and storage path. Two consequences worth stating:

- **It decouples Alex's index from marker.** The `marker-pdf` pin stays load-bearing for every self-hoster on the PDF path; it stops being load-bearing for a curated index.
- **It makes the pipeline end-to-end testable without marker for the first time.** A small synthetic curated file exercises chunk → embed → store with no model weights. Whether that becomes a real test (with a mocked embedder) or stays a manual affordance is an implementation call, but the option did not exist before this part.

The curated file needs the structure marker could not supply: a real heading hierarchy, and page markers in whatever form `chunk.py` already consumes. Document the expected format in `ingestion/README.md` precisely enough that a self-hoster could produce one for their own book — that is the entire shipped value of this part.

### 3.2 Establish the ceiling before committing to the work

Curating a 44-page book by hand is an evening at best. Before spending it, **curate two or three chapters only** — ideally `PANIC CHECKS`, `STAT CHECKS & SAVES`, and one table-heavy chapter — and score the fixtures that target those chapters against both the curated and automated versions.

That gap is the ceiling: it says how much of the remaining shortfall is chunking quality versus something else entirely (embedding model, query phrasing, the fixture labels themselves). If the gap is small, full curation is not worth the evening and Part 2's levers are the better investment. If it is large, you now have a measured reason to spend it.

This experiment is the recommended path even if you expect to curate anyway, because it is also the only way to distinguish "our chunking is bad" from "this book is hard to retrieve from."

## Part 4 — The similarity floor

**Sequencing: this must land before Part 5.** It changes what reaches the Warden, which invalidates frozen artifacts. Landing it after the re-baseline means paying for another one.

M7.2 established that `rules_lookup` has no floor: `RulesLookupService.lookup()` returns whatever `findByCosineSimilarity` gives back, and the SQL is `ORDER BY … LIMIT n` with no threshold. On a populated index the Warden therefore receives three chunks for every question, including questions the PSG cannot answer.

Use the top-1 similarity distributions from the `answerable: true` and `answerable: false` fixtures. If they separate, the floor goes between them; implement it in the service (not the SQL — keeping it in TypeScript makes it testable without a database and visible to anyone reading the service). If they overlap, **do not invent a threshold**: record the overlap in `docs/decisions.md` and leave the behavior unchanged. A floor that discards good chunks is worse than none, and the empty-result path the Warden already handles correctly is the safe default either way.

If a floor lands, note that it changes the *rate* of empty results the Warden sees, which is exactly the kind of behavioral shift Part 5 is measuring. That is intended and is why the ordering is fixed — but say so in the re-baseline's write-up, because the run then has up to three intended variables (populated index, the `roll_dice` fields per Part 4.5, and a floor if one lands) rather than one, and a reader deserves to know that before attributing a movement to any single cause.

## Part 4.5 — Land the `roll_dice` schema fields

**Sequencing: this must land before Part 5, same reasoning as Part 4.** It changes the `roll_dice` tool schema, which changes what reaches the Warden, which invalidates frozen artifacts. Landing it after the re-baseline means paying for a third one — exactly the cost `docs/decisions.md § rollType / gatedByRollId / actingEntityId on roll_dice stay deferred, but they are measurement infrastructure` was written to avoid, and the reason these fields are M7.5 scope rather than a later, standalone milestone.

### What the fields are for

Per the decisions.md entry, these aren't just a candidate fix for Warden-side sequencing — they're the precondition for *measuring* whether a fix is even needed:

- `gatedByRollId` lets `out-of-order-resolution` adjudicate the in-turn case. It currently reports `not_applicable` for any roll that resolves a gate within the same turn, because sequence numbers show what happened first, not what depended on what. The deferred-gate case (a roll spanning a turn boundary) already works structurally without this field; the in-turn case doesn't and can't.
- `actingEntityId` lets `system-rolled-player-action` attribute a Warden-side roll without a prose convention. `actorType` is `'gm'` for every Warden-side roll whether it stands in for an NPC or the player, which is exactly the distinction the check draws — and it's the last prose dependency remaining in the structural checks (`docs/decisions.md § A structural check may read event and state structure; it may not classify prose`).
- `rollType` was introduced alongside the other two in the M7.4 fixture-schema compatibility work. **Confirmed during implementation planning: there is no intended use to recover.** It appears in `docs/specs/zoltar/011-eval-harness-multi-run.md § Part 6` only as *the anticipated case* for the fixture-schema-compatibility mechanism — a hypothetical example of a field some later check might need — never mentioned in the M7.4 spec at all, and repeated as "anticipated" in `eval/checks/registry.ts`. `docs/decisions.md`'s own entry gives concrete jobs to the other two fields and conspicuously none to this one.

  **Decided:** it lands as a descriptive enum — `check` / `save` / `damage` / `panic_check` / `table` / `other` — read by no checker today, serving as telemetry and a reporting axis. The justification is the same economics that put all three fields in this milestone rather than a later one: it rides the baseline already being bought, and discovering a use for it afterwards would mean paying for a third. A field with a modest, honest purpose costs one enum; a field deferred costs a re-baseline.

### What lands

- The three fields added to the `roll_dice` tool's output schema, and to the Warden system prompt as an instruction to populate them on every roll.
- **Schema and prompt land together, not separately.** An unpopulated field reads identically to a missing one at scoring time — shipping the schema change without the prompt instruction would leave both checks at `not_applicable` regardless, and the re-baseline would have paid its cost for nothing.
- `out-of-order-resolution` and `system-rolled-player-action` updated to read the new fields for their currently-undecidable cases instead of reporting `not_applicable`. Whether this requires a `FIXTURE_SCHEMA_VERSION` bump — the M7.4 fixture schema already went 1→2 for the `applicability` field (`docs/decisions.md § Applicability is fixture-authored, keyed by checkId, never inferred from the turn's own output`) — is an implementation question this spec doesn't resolve. Check `requiresFixtureSchema` on both checks before assuming the existing fixture set is compatible as-is.

### What this does not do

- Does not itself decompose the turn loop into an agentic graph. `docs/decisions.md § Agentic graph decomposition stays deferred; dice-arbitration evidence weakens the case without closing it` treats these fields as the cheaper structural option to try *before* a graph, not a graph substitute — whether a residual survives after they land is a question for Part 5's re-baseline, not this Part.
- Does not touch `docs/schema.md`. Confirmed, not just likely: `dice_roll` events are `game_event` rows with `payload jsonb`, the same untyped column every other event type in that table uses (`docs/schema.md § Game Events`) — the three fields are new keys inside that JSONB, not new columns. No migration. `docs/tools.md` needs updating regardless, since the tool definition itself is changing.

## Part 4.6 — Warden-side query quality

**Sequencing: the prompt changes must land before Part 5, same reasoning as Parts 4 and 4.5.** Their *measurement* is read out of Part 5's own artifacts afterwards, which is the unusual shape of this Part and the reason it is worth stating plainly.

These are two `docs/roadmap.md` M7.5 bullets that the first draft of this spec omitted. Recovered here rather than moved to M8.1, because M8.1 is sequenced after M8 and both of these change the Warden prompt — deferring them means either a fourth baseline or leaving a measured 37.8% failure rate untouched for two milestones.

### What problem each one attacks

Parts 1–3 improve what the index *contains* and how it is *chunked*. Neither touches the other half of a retrieval failure: a query that could not have succeeded no matter how good the index was. `docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S8.3` and `§ S9` split those into two buckets across 596 real recorded queries, and the split is the whole reason these are two levers and not one:

- **Wrong word — 45.6% of the 344 out-of-corpus queries.** The Warden asks for `perception` where the book says `Intellect`, `diagnosis` where it says `pathology`. A vocabulary layer fixes these. `§ S5.3` measured the size of the effect directly: swapping `perception`→`intellect` moved a target from 9th to 4th, and cutting to core terms moved it to 1st. Dense retrieval bridges the vocabulary gap **partially, not for free** — that was the assumption `§ S5.3` overturned.
- **Concept-absent — 37.8%.** The Warden asks about flanking, opposed rolls, difficulty classes, suppressive fire. Mothership has none of them. **No retrieval fix exists for this bucket**, at any chunking quality, with any embedding model. The only intervention that works is upstream: stop generating the query.

**Vocabulary bridging** targets the first bucket. **The mechanical-model primer** targets the second. They are not substitutes and neither subsumes the other — `§ S9`'s headline finding is precisely that "neither dominates, and they are not substitutes for each other."

### What lands

- **Vocabulary bridging** — prompt-side guidance steering the Warden's `rules_lookup` phrasing toward book vocabulary and toward fewer, more distinctive terms. Per `docs/decisions.md § Query preprocessing for rules_lookup…`, the prompt-side option is free and the per-system synonym/thesaurus table is real ongoing per-system authoring cost. **The prompt version ships; the table does not.** Whether the table is still wanted is a question this Part answers with a number.
- **The mechanical-model primer** — a block teaching Mothership's actual resolution model: roll-under-stat, no DC or target number, no opposed rolls, no flanking, no initiative in the D&D sense. Distinct from vocabulary bridging in kind, not degree: bridging rephrases a question the book can answer, the primer prevents a question the book cannot.
- **Both land in the same prompt as Part 4.5's `roll_dice` instructions**, in one prompt revision. Three prompt changes in one hash is not a discipline failure — it is the same batching argument the `roll_dice` fields themselves ride on, and separating them would buy three baselines to isolate variables that are not in competition.

### The instrument

The measurement does **not** come from `task eval:retrieval`. That harness scores *frozen fixture queries* against the index; these levers change the *distribution of queries the Warden emits*, which a fixed query set cannot observe. Measuring them needs Warden-generated queries.

Those already exist, at no additional spend. `warden-output.json` is the full serialized `TurnExecutionResult` (`docs/decisions.md § warden-output.json is the full serialized TurnExecutionResult`), whose `telemetry` field is the `adventure_telemetry` row, whose `payload.rulesLookups` records the query text of every `rules_lookup` call in that turn. **Verified against the frozen `claude-sonnet-5__97feadbd__2026-07-29T15-40-17Z` run: 146 artifacts, 134 lookups, 102 distinct queries.** The empty index of the M7 baselines is no obstacle — `RulesLookupService.lookup()` embeds and queries regardless of whether the index has rows, deliberately, because "every lookup attempt is telemetry input for ingestion prioritization."

So the before-number is already on disk, for both models, on the same fixtures, and the after-number falls out of Part 5's run. That is a genuine paired comparison rather than a cross-corpus one against `§ S9`'s `unicorn-artifacts` sample.

**What is scored:** the mechanically-computable half of `§ S9`'s method — the share of emitted queries carrying at least one term absent from the corpus, checked against the `rules_chunk` lexeme set. `§ S9.1`'s double-stemming bug is a specified hazard, not a discovered one: check presence with `to_tsquery('simple', …)` or by comparing lexeme sets directly, never by re-stemming a lexeme `websearch_to_tsquery` already stemmed, which silently reported `surpris` → `surpri` as absent from its own tsvector.

**What is not automated:** the wrong-word / concept-absent split within that share. `§ S9` did it as a hand audit of 100 distinct out-of-corpus terms and that remains the honest method at this scale. The automated rate is the headline; the split is a paragraph of judgement in the findings session, as it was in `§ S9.3`.

**Reading the result.** A fall in the out-of-corpus rate is the effect both levers exist to produce, and the two buckets move for different reasons — so report the rate for each model, before and after, and say which bucket moved. A rate that does not move is a finding: it says prompt-side guidance is insufficient and the per-system synonym table is the remaining option, which is exactly the decision `docs/roadmap.md`'s bullet defers to this measurement.

**One caution.** Retrieval quality also changed between the two runs, and a better index shrinks the out-of-corpus *consequence* without shrinking the *rate*. The rate is computed over query text against the corpus lexeme set, so it is insensitive to chunking — but say so in the write-up, because a reader will reasonably wonder.

## Part 5 — Eval re-baseline

Inherited from M7.2's spec Part 6.1. Full corpus, uniform N (10 as of the 2026-07-29 calibration — keep it uniform, per `docs/eval-methodology.md`'s adaptive-N hazard), both models (`claude-sonnet-4-6` and `claude-sonnet-5`), compared against the re-scored `88fa84bd8329` runs `claude-sonnet-4-6__97feadbd__2026-07-29T10-51-26Z` and `claude-sonnet-5__97feadbd__2026-07-29T15-40-17Z`.

**Run before Part 6's fixtures land.** `corpusVersion` is a content hash over every file in `eval/fixtures/` (`eval/corpus-version.ts`), and `eval:compare` warns on any cross-corpus pairing — deliberately, because that is the silent-poisoning case the hash exists to prevent. Baseline against the corpus as it stands, then capture.

**Verify the index is populated in the database the harness points at**, before spending anything. Structurally this holds — `eval:run` uses `--env-file=.env` and its scratch adventure resolves the same `mothership` `game_system` row ingestion writes under — but a re-baseline against an empty index measures nothing and looks exactly like a successful run. `task eval:retrieval` against the same `.env` is the cheapest possible check and should be the last thing done before the spend.

**The comparison is index-vs-index, not model-vs-model — with the `roll_dice` fields, the two Part 4.6 prompt levers, and possibly a floor as intended companion changes, not confounds.** Same corpus, same N, same grader. Unlike an accidental confound, every one of these is deliberate: each is a change that alters what reaches the Warden, and shipping any of them without this re-baseline would just relocate its cost to a later one rather than closing the question with this one.

**Count them out loud in the write-up.** The run carries up to **five** intended variables — the populated index, the three `roll_dice` fields, the vocabulary-bridging prompt block, the mechanical-model primer, and a similarity floor if Part 4 landed one — against a single changed prompt hash. That is a lot to attribute a movement to, and the honest posture is to say so up front rather than let a reader assume one cause. Two things make it tolerable rather than reckless: the levers attack disjoint failure modes (retrieval content, roll sequencing, query phrasing), and each carries its own narrower instrument that does not depend on this run's rates — `task eval:retrieval` for the index, the two structural checkers' verdicts for the `roll_dice` fields, the out-of-corpus query rate for the prompt levers. Where a per-lever instrument exists, cite it instead of attributing to the aggregate.

Update `Current baseline N` in `docs/eval-methodology.md` if applicability or variance shifted. Rules availability plausibly changes reach-for-dice behaviour, which moves applicability denominators on the roll-shaped checks.

## Part 6 — Dedicated playtest and fixture capture

Inherited from M7.2's spec Part 6.2. A real Mothership session against the final index, reviewed with `task playtest:review`.

It answers two questions the harness cannot. **Did the index actually help?** — the harness replays frozen turns from adventures that ran without rules; it can show whether behaviour on those turns changed, not whether a live session goes better. **Does it supply the fixtures M7.4 is still waiting on?** — `MISSING-CANON-CAPTURE`, `UNSURFACED-CHECK`, `OVER-RESOLUTION`, and `SCENE-JUMP` each sit at a single fixture. Capture with `task eval:capture-fixture`.

Read the `rulesLookups` telemetry specifically. This is the first playtest in the project's history where those lookups return anything, so it is also the first real evidence about query-style distribution — feed anything surprising back into the retrieval fixture set as new `warden-observed` entries, *after* the milestone closes rather than during Part 2's frozen window.

**One caution, and it is the point rather than boilerplate.** A single session is not guaranteed to exhibit all four failure modes, and a fixture authored from a turn that did not actually exhibit its tag is worse than a missing fixture — it pins a check to behaviour that was never wrong, permanently and invisibly. If a tag stays at one instance, that is evidence about how rare the mode is: record it, and leave M7.4's fixture bullet open rather than closing it with a manufactured instance.

## Deferrals Introduced in M7.5

### Embedding model upgrade as a retrieval lever

`voyage-4` and `voyage-4-large` are the documented step-ups from `voyage-4-lite` (`docs/rules-ingestion.md § Step 5`), all three at 1024 dimensions, so a swap is a config change rather than a migration. It is deliberately not a Part 2 lever: it changes the runtime `VOYAGE_EMBED_MODEL` as well as ingestion, costs a full re-embed, and forces its own Warden re-baseline. Revisit if chunking work plateaus below the bar.

### Section headings indexed *together with* reference-card dedup

Round 3 (`docs/rules-extraction-findings.md § S19`) measured heading
inclusion doing two opposite things at once: it recovered printed page 12,
whose tables all extract empty so the page was absent from the index
entirely, and it handed printed page 44 — a compressed restatement of
pp.18–21 — the topic labels it needed to outrank the body pages it
restates. Net: −1 fixture on recall, so the lever was reverted.

The obvious next experiment is the two together: headings on, physical 43
dropped or deduped. `§ S19.5` predicts it clears the outstanding bar metric,
recovering `rq-015` while returning `rq-020` / `rq-025` / `rq-026`.

**Deliberately not run, and the prediction is the reason.** Three rounds was
the budget and the stopping rule had already fired on round 3. A milestone
that appends one more round because the last one was interesting is the
failure mode `§ The stopping rule` exists to prevent, and a round run
*because its outcome is predicted* is the worst version of it — the point of
a measurement is that it can come out the other way. It costs about three
minutes and a few dozen Voyage calls whenever someone decides to spend them
deliberately.

### The per-system synonym / thesaurus table

Part 4.6 ships the free half of the vocabulary-bridging question — prompt-side guidance — and measures what it bought. The table is the paid half: real ongoing per-system authoring cost, per `docs/decisions.md § Query preprocessing for rules_lookup…`, and it cannot be evaluated honestly until the free option's ceiling is known. If Part 4.6's out-of-corpus rate barely moves, the table becomes the obvious next lever with a measured reason behind it; if the rate falls, it may never be needed. Either way the decision is recorded in M7.5 and the work is not done here.

### A second curated book

If curation proves its value on the PSG, the Warden's Operations Manual is the obvious next candidate. Out of scope here — one curated book is enough to learn whether the approach is worth generalizing.

### Retrieval fixtures for non-Mothership systems

The fixture set is Mothership-specific by construction (page labels, chapter names). A second system needs its own set, authored the same way. Not a reusable asset, and not a reason to delay this one.

### Automated chunk-quality regression

Once a bar exists and is met, a regression check that fails when retrieval degrades becomes conceivable. It needs a populated index and Voyage calls, so it cannot be a CI job as things stand — it would need a cached-embedding fixture harness, which is its own design problem. Revisit if chunking changes become frequent enough that manual re-scoring is the bottleneck.
